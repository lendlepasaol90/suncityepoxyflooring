Residential epoxy flooring · Sun City, AZ

Seamless interior floors that are simple to care for.

When a coated floor makes sense indoors

Most people think of epoxy as a garage floor, but the same technology makes an excellent interior surface in the right rooms. Wherever there is a concrete slab under worn carpet, cracked tile or tired vinyl, a coating can become the finished floor — one continuous surface with no seams, no grout and nothing to trap dust or pet hair.

In Sun City homes, the rooms where this works best tend to be the practical ones. The laundry room, where a washer hose occasionally lets go. The entry from the garage, where shoes come in with grit from the driveway. The Arizona room, which sits somewhere between indoors and outdoors and has to handle sandy feet and sliding patio doors. Craft rooms, workshops and casitas round out the list.

These are rooms that see real use and benefit from a floor you can simply wipe clean. A coated floor delivers that, and it can look surprisingly elegant when finished in a soft color with a satin sheen.

Benefits you’ll notice day to day

Nothing for spills to soak into

Water, coffee and cleaning products stay on the surface until you wipe them up. A coated cove edge keeps water from creeping under walls or cabinets.

Easy, gentle cleaning

A soft dust mop and an occasional damp mop are all it takes. No grout to scrub, no carpet to shampoo.

Better footing

We can add a fine texture to the topcoat so the floor grips well, even in socks or when slightly damp.

A cooler feel in summer

Concrete stays cooler underfoot than carpet — a welcome change in an Arizona room during the hot months.

Long service life

When a busy path eventually dulls, the topcoat can be refreshed without replacing the floor.

Fewer allergens

With no fibers or gaps, there are fewer places for dust and pollen to settle.

Choosing a finish for an interior room

Interior floors are about comfort and style as much as durability. These are the finishes we recommend most for living spaces.

Solid color, satin sheen
Calm and understated. Warm grays, greige and sand tones pair well with most décor, and satin hides footprints better than gloss.
Fine flake
Small flakes in a light scatter add gentle texture and hide crumbs and dust, with a look closer to terrazzo than to a garage floor.
Metallic
For a den, home bar or entry, a metallic finish adds depth and movement reminiscent of polished stone.
Cove base
Curving the finish a short way up the wall leaves no seam at the base, which is especially handy beside a washer.

How we work inside your home

  1. 1

    Plan the project together

    We discuss the room, how you use it, and the schedule. Appliances and furniture are moved out, and we plan how you’ll get around the house while the floor cures.

  2. 2

    Remove old flooring

    Carpet, tile or vinyl comes up first, and any adhesive is ground away so the coating bonds to clean concrete.

  3. 3

    Contain dust and odor

    We seal doorways with plastic, run vacuum-connected grinders and set up ventilation. The rest of your home stays clean.

  4. 4

    Grind, repair and level

    The slab is ground smooth, and cracks or low spots are filled so the finished surface is even.

  5. 5

    Apply color and topcoat

    Primer, color coat and topcoat are applied with cure time between each layer. Cove edges are finished by hand.

  6. 6

    Return the room to you

    Fresh air keeps flowing while it cures, and we tell you when it’s ready for footsteps, furniture and appliances.

Why interior coatings call for experience

Inside a home, every detail is on display. A solid satin floor in a laundry room or an Arizona room shows any unevenness in the slab, any repair that wasn’t feathered smoothly, and any line where the roller overlapped. The skill is in preparation and careful, consistent application — work that takes patience rather than speed.

There’s also the matter of protecting a lived-in home. We cover cabinets and baseboards, keep dust contained, and plan around pets and daily routines. These considerations don’t come up in a garage, and they make a real difference to how pleasant the project feels.

Interior floors in Sun City homes

Many homes here are single-level and built on a slab, which makes them well suited to coated floors. Some older slabs were poured without a vapor barrier underneath, though, which can allow a little moisture to rise into the floor. Our moisture meter comes out before any price is given, and a vapor-tolerant primer goes into the plan if readings are high.

Arizona rooms deserve special mention. They often have large windows or sliding doors that let in a lot of sun. We use UV-stable topcoats in these rooms to keep colors from shifting, and we often suggest a lighter color that stays cool and keeps the room feeling bright.

If you’re updating a room with mobility in mind, a coated floor can help. It creates smooth transitions with no loose rugs or raised grout lines, and texture can be added for extra grip. We’re happy to discuss threshold details and transitions with you during the estimate.

A closer look at Arizona rooms and enclosed patios

An Arizona room is one of the most useful spaces in a desert home, and one of the hardest on flooring. Many began life as a covered patio that was later enclosed, so the slab may sit a step lower than the house, slope gently toward the yard, or carry old outdoor paint. Carpet in these rooms tends to fade and hold sand; tile can crack where the old patio slab meets the house foundation.

A coating handles these quirks well. We grind away old patio paint, address the joint where the two slabs meet, and can build up low areas so the floor feels level. A light, UV-stable color keeps the room bright and cool, and a fine texture gives good grip where people step in from the pool or the garden. The result is a floor that feels intentional rather than a patio with walls around it.

Caring for an interior epoxy floor

Use a microfiber dust mop for everyday cleaning, and damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner as needed. Skip oil soaps, waxes and vinegar-heavy mixtures; they can leave a film or dull the finish over time.

Felt pads on chair and table legs protect the topcoat from scratches. A doormat at the entry from the garage catches grit before it reaches the floor. With these small habits, an interior coating keeps its good looks for many years.

What an interior project usually costs — and why

Interior floors are priced by the room rather than by a simple square-foot figure, because the work varies so much. Removing old tile and thinset takes more time than pulling up carpet. A slab with several cracks or an uneven patch needs more preparation than one in good shape. Cove edges, metallic finishes and moisture-mitigating primers each add a step.

During the estimate we walk you through what your room needs and why. The written estimate lists each item separately, so you can see where the effort goes and decide whether any options — like a cove base or added texture — are worth including.

Questions we’re often asked

Will there be a strong odor in the house?

We use lower-odor products for interior work wherever they meet the performance needed, ventilate during application and keep the room closed off. Most households are comfortable staying home throughout.

Can a coated floor go over radiant heat or in-floor plumbing?

Coatings sit on top of the slab, so they don’t interfere with what’s inside it. We grind lightly and carefully in these situations and avoid any cutting.

Is it cold underfoot in winter?

Concrete feels cooler than carpet. Most people enjoy that in summer, and a rug or two in the seating area keeps things cozy during the short winter.

Can you match the color to my cabinets or walls?

We have a wide range of standard colors and can often tint closer to a specific shade. Bring a paint chip or cabinet sample to the estimate.

How long until I can use the room?

Light foot traffic is usually fine within a day of the topcoat. Furniture and appliances typically return a day or two later.

Can you do just one room?

Absolutely. Many of our interior projects are single laundry rooms or entries.

Will an interior coating affect my home’s resale?

A well-finished, durable floor in a laundry room, entry or Arizona room is generally seen as an upgrade over worn carpet or cracked tile. As with any finish, buyers’ tastes vary, so we recommend timeless, neutral colors in shared living areas.
