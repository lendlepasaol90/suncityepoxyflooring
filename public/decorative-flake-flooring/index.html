Speckled flake floors that look tidy and feel sure underfoot.

What a flake floor is

A decorative flake floor is a coating system with thin, colored vinyl flakes sandwiched between two clear-wearing layers. First, a base coat is applied to freshly ground concrete. Before it sets, handfuls of flakes are thrown across the surface until it is covered to the level you want. After the base hardens, we knock off the flakes that didn’t stick, vacuum thoroughly and roll a clear protective layer over everything.

The finished surface has a speckled, granite-like appearance. Up close you see individual flakes in several colors; from across the room they blend into a single tone. That blending effect is what makes flake floors so practical: they disguise dust, sand, small scuffs and the odd drip far better than a solid color can.

Why homeowners love flake

Looks clean longer

The multi-color speckle hides the fine dust that blows in under the garage door, so the floor looks tidy between sweeps.

Comfortable, secure footing

Flakes give a mild texture that grips better than smooth concrete or gloss paint. Extra grit can be added where needed.

Tough and resilient

The flakes add body to the coating, helping it handle dropped tools, cart tires and heavy storage bins.

Easy to repair

Should something heavy ever nick the surface, a small flake repair blends in almost invisibly — far easier than touching up a solid color.

Endless color choices

Choose from quiet neutrals, warm desert tones or crisp grays — or mix a custom blend to match your home.

Choosing your blend

Picking a flake blend is the part most people enjoy. A few practical pointers make the decision easier.

  • Match the dust, not the décor. Blends with some tan or beige in the mix hide Arizona dust best. Pure grays and whites show it a little sooner.
  • Consider the light. Garages lit by a single bulb benefit from lighter blends that bounce light around.
  • Look at your stucco and doors. Echoing a shade from the stucco or trim makes the garage feel like part of the house.
  • See it in person. Sample boards look different on the floor than they do in a photo, so we bring them to every estimate.

Flake size and coverage

Two choices shape the character of a flake floor: how big the flakes are, and how densely they cover the base.

Standard ¼-inch flake
The classic garage look. Large enough to hide the most dirt and give the most texture.
Fine ⅛-inch or micro flake
Smaller flakes create a softer, more refined pattern well suited to laundry rooms, entries and Arizona rooms.
Full coverage
Flakes completely cover the base coat for maximum hide, texture and durability. Our standard recommendation for garages.
Partial coverage
With fewer flakes, more of the base color peeks through. It reads cleaner and more contemporary but shows dirt sooner.

How we install a flake floor

  1. 1

    Prepare the concrete

    Diamond grinding removes old paint, sealers and the weak top layer so the coating can bond securely.

  2. 2

    Repair imperfections

    Cracks and chips are filled and smoothed. Most minor flaws disappear under full flake coverage.

  3. 3

    Apply the base coat

    A tinted base coat is rolled out in sections. Its color subtly influences the finished look.

  4. 4

    Broadcast the flakes

    Flakes are thrown upward so they fall evenly, working across the floor until the base is covered.

  5. 5

    Scrape and vacuum

    After curing, standing flakes are knocked down and the floor is thoroughly cleaned for a smooth feel.

  6. 6

    Seal with a clear topcoat

    A UV-stable topcoat locks the flakes in place and sets the sheen. Optional grit is added here.

The details that separate a good flake floor from a great one

Two flake floors can use identical materials and still look very different. The most common flaw is uneven broadcasting — patches where the base color peeks through, or streaks where flakes piled up. Even coverage takes practice and a methodical approach, working the floor in planned sections.

Scraping matters just as much. Skip it and the floor feels rough, catches mops and wears the topcoat unevenly. Done well, the surface has a pleasant, gentle texture. Finally, the topcoat has to be applied thickly enough to fully encapsulate the flakes. We take care at each of these steps, because they decide how the floor feels every time you walk on it.

Flake floors in Sun City

Here, flake floors are a natural fit for golf cart garages and multi-purpose spaces. A garage that holds a car, a cart, a workbench and an exercise bike needs a floor that tolerates all of it while looking presentable when the door is open to the street.

Flake also works outdoors on covered patios when paired with a UV-stable, textured topcoat. Lighter blends stay more comfortable in the summer sun and are less likely to show the reddish desert dust that settles on outdoor surfaces.

For interior rooms, finer flakes in soft colors give a polished look that suits the bright, airy style of many homes in the area. It’s a durable option for laundry rooms and entries that feels at home alongside cabinetry and tile elsewhere in the house.

Caring for a flake floor

Flake floors are among the easiest surfaces to maintain. A soft broom or dust mop removes everyday grit. For a deeper clean, mop with warm water and a small amount of pH-neutral cleaner, then rinse and squeegee dry. Avoid acidic cleaners and stiff wire brushes.

If something heavy does chip the floor, don’t worry. Call us and we can patch and blend the area so it is hard to spot.

Flake versus other garage finishes

If you’re weighing flake against a solid color or a metallic finish, think about how the space is used. Solid colors look sleek and make it easy to find a dropped screw, but they show dust, footprints and tire marks more quickly. Metallic floors are striking but are better suited to interior rooms with less grit and traffic.

Flake sits in the sweet spot for most garages: forgiving, durable, comfortable to walk on and attractive enough to leave the door open. That balance is why it accounts for the majority of the garage floors we install.

Flake beyond the garage

Flake floors aren’t only for parking. In laundry rooms and utility spaces, a fine flake in a soft neutral hides lint and detergent drips while giving a little traction near the washer. In entries from the garage, it stands up to grit from shoes and looks far more finished than painted concrete.

Workshops and hobby rooms are another good fit. Woodworkers appreciate that sawdust sweeps up cleanly, and crafters like that paint or glue drips can be scraped off the sealed surface. Covered patios and Arizona rooms round out the list, provided the topcoat is UV-stable and the texture is suited to wet feet.

What affects the price of a flake floor

The size of the space is only part of the story. Preparation is the biggest variable: a clean, unpainted slab grinds quickly, while one with old paint, heavy oil staining or numerous cracks takes more time. Full coverage uses more flake than partial coverage, and custom blends or finer flakes can cost a little more. A one-day polyaspartic system uses faster, more specialized materials than a multi-day epoxy system.

Your written estimate lists each of these items so you can see exactly what you’re paying for, and compare our proposal fairly with any other you receive.

Questions we’re often asked

Do the flakes ever come loose?

No. Each flake is set into the base layer and then covered by the clear coat, so there’s nothing for it to come loose from.

Is a flake floor rough on bare feet?

A well-scraped and sealed flake floor has a gentle orange-peel feel and is comfortable to walk on. Added grit makes it a little coarser.

Can I create a custom color blend?

Yes. We can mix blends from individual flake colors to suit your home. Bring a paint chip or photo to the estimate.

Will the colors fade near the garage door?

Our UV-stable topcoats protect the flakes from fading and yellowing, even where sunlight reaches the floor.

Can flake be used on a patio?

Yes, with an outdoor-rated, UV-stable topcoat and added texture for wet conditions.

Can you coat just one bay of my garage?

We can, but most people prefer the whole floor for a consistent look. If budget is a concern, we can discuss options such as partial coverage that keep costs down without a visible seam.

How thick is a flake floor?

Thickness varies by system, but a full-broadcast flake floor is substantially thicker than paint or a DIY kit, which is a big part of why it lasts.
