Bringing tired outdoor concrete back to life

Outdoor concrete leads a hard life in the desert. It bakes in the sun all summer, absorbs sprinkler water and its minerals, collects leaf and pollen stains, and soaks up any grease that escapes the grill. Over time a patio can look blotchy, faded and cracked — even if it’s structurally sound.

A patio coating gives the concrete a fresh, even finish in a color that complements your home. More importantly, it seals the surface so future spills and stains stay on top, where they can be rinsed away. With the right texture, a coated patio is also safer to walk on when it’s wet from the pool, the hose or a summer storm.

We coat covered patios, open patios, pool decks, front porches and entries, walkways, carports and outdoor kitchens. Covered patios are the most common starting point, since they’re protected from the harshest sun and offer the widest range of color choices.

What a patio coating improves

Safer footing

A fine grit in the final layer keeps damp feet steady, which matters most beside the pool and at the steps into the house.

Cooler surface

Light, reflective colors absorb less heat than dark concrete stains or paint, keeping the patio more comfortable.

Stain resistance

Grill grease, food spills, hard-water marks and leaf stains rinse off a sealed surface.

Even, uniform color

Hides old patches, mismatched repairs and discoloration under one consistent finish.

Sun-stable finish

Outdoor-rated topcoats resist fading and the yellowing that ordinary epoxy develops in sunlight.

Outdoor finish options

Textured flake
Decorative flakes sealed under a textured topcoat. Light blends hide dust and stay cooler.
Solid color with grit
A clean, single color that coordinates with your stucco and trim, with fine grit for traction.
Stone-look blends
Multi-color flakes that resemble natural granite — a natural partner for desert landscaping.
Clear protective sealer
If you love the pattern in stamped or colored concrete, a clear protective sealer guards it while leaving the design on show.

How we coat a patio or deck

  1. 1

    Assess the slab and drainage

    We check for cracks, low spots that hold water, existing sealers and how water drains away from the house.

  2. 2

    Clean and grind

    Outdoor slabs are cleaned of organic stains, then ground to remove old sealers and open the surface.

  3. 3

    Repair and detail joints

    Cracks are filled and expansion joints are treated so the slab can move without damaging the coating.

  4. 4

    The base coat is applied, followed by flakes or pigment according to your design.

  5. 5

    Textured topcoat

    A UV-stable topcoat with traction grit finishes the surface.

  6. 6

    Cure and enjoy

    We’ll tell you when you can walk on it and when to return furniture, planters and the grill.

Why many patio coatings fail — and how we prevent it

Most failed patio coatings we’re asked to fix share a handful of causes. An interior epoxy was used outdoors and turned yellow and chalky. The surface had no texture and became slick when wet. The coating was applied over an old sealer it couldn’t bond to. Or it was rolled straight across expansion joints and cracked along them within a season.

We avoid each of these by choosing outdoor-rated, UV-stable materials, grinding away old sealers, building texture into the finish and detailing every joint properly. None of these steps is expensive at installation time, but together they make the difference between a coating that lasts and one that peels.

Choosing a comfortable color

In the summer sun, color affects comfort more than people expect. Dark surfaces absorb much more heat and can be uncomfortable for bare feet by mid-afternoon. Light tans, soft grays and creams stay noticeably cooler. If your patio gets direct afternoon sun, we’ll steer you toward the lighter end of the palette.

Dust is another consideration. Fine desert dust settles on every outdoor surface. It stands out on pale and deep colors alike; speckled mid-tones keep it out of sight. Finally, consider the surrounding colors — stucco, roof tile, landscape rock and patio furniture — so the coating feels like a natural part of the space.

Pool decks deserve extra attention

Around a pool, the surface needs to grip wet feet without being rough on skin. We blend a fine grit into a pale top layer and try a sample patch with bare feet before finishing the entire deck. The coating also needs to tolerate chlorine or salt water, which our outdoor systems are designed to handle.

Salt-water pools in particular benefit from a sealed deck. Salt can crystallize inside bare concrete and gradually wear the surface. A coating keeps salt on top, where a quick rinse removes it. We also detail the edges carefully where the deck meets the pool coping and drains.

Patios and entries in Sun City

For much of the year, the patio is the most-used room in many Sun City homes. Mornings and evenings from fall through spring are perfect for sitting outside, and a fresh, comfortable surface makes the space far more inviting.

Summer monsoons bring sudden downpours and dust. Textured, sealed surfaces drain and rinse clean easily. Front entries and walkways are worth considering too — a coating with good texture provides secure footing at the front door, and it gives the home’s entrance a crisp, cared-for look.

Because many homes here are single-level, the step from the house to the patio is often small or even flush. We’ll take care to keep thresholds smooth and avoid creating any new lips or edges at doorways.

Keeping your patio looking its best

Upkeep amounts to clearing leaves with a blower or broom and giving the slab a quick hose-down now and then. For grill grease, use a mild degreaser and a soft deck brush. If you use a pressure washer, keep the nozzle at a wide fan setting and a comfortable distance from the surface.

Put rubber or plastic glides on metal furniture legs, and set potted plants on feet or saucers so water doesn’t pool beneath them. With that minimal attention, a coated patio will look good for many years.

Planning around the seasons

Outdoor coatings are best applied when temperatures are moderate and the forecast is dry. Fall, winter and spring are ideal in Sun City, and that happens to be when most people want to use their patios. In summer, we schedule work for early mornings and keep a close eye on monsoon forecasts, rescheduling if a storm threatens before the coating has cured.

If you’re planning a gathering or a holiday visit, let us know when we schedule. We’ll build in enough cure time so the patio is ready before your guests arrive.

Front entries and walkways

The path to your front door sets the tone for the whole home. A stained, cracked walkway or a faded entry slab can make an otherwise well-kept property look tired. A coating gives these areas a crisp, uniform finish that matches or complements the patio, and the texture provides secure footing in all conditions.

Entries also make good small projects if you want to see how a coating looks before committing to a larger patio. Many customers start with the front entry and return later for the patio or garage once they’ve lived with the finish.

Questions we’re often asked

Will the patio get too hot in summer?

Any surface in direct sun gets warm. Lighter colors stay noticeably cooler, and we’ll recommend shades suited to how much sun your patio receives.

Can a coated deck work next to the pool?

It works well when the finish has proper grip. A gritty finish keeps wet feet secure, and the outdoor materials we use hold up to chlorine and salt.

Can you coat my stamped concrete?

Two routes: keep the existing pattern under a clear sealer, or grind it smooth and start over with a brand-new finish.

When can we sit out on the patio again?

Most patios welcome gentle foot traffic within a day; chairs and tables can go back a little after that, depending on the weather.

What about the cracks in my patio?

Cracks get filled during prep. Where the ground is still shifting, it may reappear, and we’ll point out any that look active.

Can you coat the front walkway too?

Yes. Walkways and front entries are popular additions, especially for the added footing a textured finish provides.

Does the coating hold up to sprinkler overspray?

Yes. The sealed surface resists hard-water staining, and mineral spots rinse away more easily than on bare concrete. Adjusting sprinkler heads to keep water off the patio also helps.
